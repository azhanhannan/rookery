# Opening trainer, round 2: learning specialist responds to the coach

*19 Oct 2026. Reply to `round1-coach.md`.*

## (a) Where we agree

We agree on most of it. Pause needs a frozen schedule, and we both flag that as the biggest backfire risk.
"Resting" is a better label than "Paused". Neither of us wants the backlog total on the main screen. We both
want a line finished before the session stops, no new lines while behind on reviews, and random spread on the
long intervals. A miss should not reset a card to day 1. A Hint should hold the card at its stage, neither
advancing it nor counting as a miss. Positions shared by same-colour repertoires should count and be drilled
once. The summary should lead with what he remembered and never show a percentage.

The coach caught one thing I missed: misses in **Practice and Weakest mode must not touch the schedule** at
all. I only protected moves that weren't due. I adopt the coach's rule.

## (b) Where I differ, and how I'd settle it

- **Repertoire size (30–45 moves, one White opening active, Caro-Kann trimmed to 3–4 lines).** The app should
  **recommend trimming, never enforce it.** Autonomy is what keeps an anxious adult coming back. An app that
  deletes or locks his openings becomes one more authority judging him. What the app enforces is the *load*,
  through the caps and the learning budget. The content is his choice. The recommendation should come once, in
  the coach's words, with a one-click "Rest" and an "Ask me later" option. It should never come back as a
  standing warning.
- **The cap unit: lines vs graded moves, 15 vs 12.** We are closer than it looks. The coach's "finish the line,
  then stop" is my "soft at line end". The question is what to *count*. Count **graded due moves**, because
  lines vary from 2 to 12 moves and the debt sits in moves. Show it in lines: "about 4 lines left today". I
  accept **15**. With shared positions deduplicated and moves that weren't due no longer counted, 15 is about 4–5
  lines, or 8–10 minutes.
- **A miss drops one stage or two?** I accept **one stage** (21→7, 7→3). The coach's argument holds once the
  missed move is asked again at the end of the line and moves that weren't due can't lapse. I add one condition:
  log retention privately. If first-time recall on due moves falls below 80% over 30 days, switch to two stages.
- **New material: one line/day or 6 moves/day?** These are the same rule with different units. Combined: **one
  new line per day, at most 8 new moves.** A longer line is split at a natural point, such as after castling.
  Learning is **blocked while today's reviews are unfinished, or while the 7-day forecast is above the cap.**
  Before learning, show the cost: "This line adds about 15 reviews next week."
- **Asking him to choose between conflicting repertoires.** Agree. Prompt **at most twice**: once when a
  conflicting repertoire is created, and once after 3 mix-ups at the same position. Always offer "Keep both".
  If he keeps both, show the repertoire's name ("Ruy Lopez: move 3") before the move where they split. Space
  out drilling of the two answers to that position so they don't come up on the same day. Reminding him which
  opening he's in before the move works better than correcting him afterwards.
- **Showing the line's idea before its moves.** Strongly agree. One sentence of idea gives the moves something
  to hang on. I'd add one thing: at the end of the line, ask "What's the plan here?" with two choices and no
  grading. Recalling the plan himself, not just reading it, is what makes it stick. Writing his own plan
  should stay optional, never a required step.
- **"Play from here" against a bot.** Agree. Using the position in a game helps it stick. It must be **ungraded
  and off the schedule**, against a bot set weaker than him, and framed as "try the plan", not "win". A loss
  right after a drill would undo the calm we're building.
- **Re-spreading the 70.** Agree to a **one-time re-spread over 5 days** when the caps ship. Order the spread by
  **depth first (moves 1–4 before deep sidelines), then by lowest stage**. That is the coach's "protect the
  trunk" rule, and it's also the most common positions first. Credit for remembering a late card (my §2c)
  then covers later backlogs.

## (c) Joint build order

| # | Build | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Caps + one-time re-spread + no backlog total on screen | 15 graded due moves/day, line always finished, 70 spread over 5 days by depth then stage, shown as "Today: N" |
| 2 | Lapses that don't hurt | Miss = drop 1 stage; asked again once at line end, ungraded; moves not due and all Practice/Weakest misses never change the schedule; "Tricky spots" judged on the last 5 attempts |
| 3 | Resting (pause) | Due dates shifted forward by the days rested; on resume, at most 7 due moves/day from that repertoire for 7 days; excluded from every count |
| 4 | Wording + Hint | "Not yet. Here it is: Bb5," plus the line's plan comment; Hint press 1 shows the piece, press 2 the square; a hint holds the card at its stage, next review after the previous rung's interval |
| 5 | Learning budget + forecast | 1 line/day, at most 8 moves; blocked while behind; review cost shown before learning; the from-memory pass right after the demo is ungraded, and the graded recall comes at session end |
| 6 | Spread + late credit | ±15% random spread on intervals of 3 days or more, landing on the lightest day; a late but correct answer earns credit for the full gap |
| 7 | Other repertoire's move + conflict choice | Named "That's your Italian move", card holds its stage; choose-one prompt at most twice; name chip shown before the split |
| 8 | Coaching layer | Idea first, plan check at line end, play from here (bot ~200 below him), one-time size check (aim ~40 moves), deviation branches 1–3 moves, "came up in N of your games" |

We both measure success the same way: he opens the trainer tomorrow without dread.
