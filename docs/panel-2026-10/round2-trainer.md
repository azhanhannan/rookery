## (a) Where I agree

- **Aina, "Try again" and plain-words lines:** yes. Retrying the position is self-review in miniature: the student finds the move before seeing it. One sentence a 10-year-old understands teaches more than "+1.3".
- **Aina, hanging-piece warning in casual games:** I agree, with conditions (see c). Undefended pieces cause most losses under 800.
- **Daniel, the mistake-to-puzzle loop fed by bulk import:** we arrived at the same idea separately, so it's my #1. Your opening report ("−1.2 by move 12 in 7 of 9 games") is exactly the kind of diagnosis I give students by hand.
- **Farid, analysis board with a variation tree and full PGN:** agreed, and it serves my teaching too. I prepare lessons as annotated PGN, and today Rookery would delete my comments. "Analyse deeper here" is cheap and honest.
- **Everyone on rated games:** takebacks and helpers stay out of rated games. All four of us agree on that.

## (b) Where I disagree

- **Daniel, premoves:** they're fine for blitz, but they don't help anyone improve, and for my juniors they build a habit of moving without looking. Build them later, as an opt-in setting.
- **Aina, the daily-puzzle streak:** a streak of *days you opened the app* trains you to open the app, nothing more. Count days you trained (one puzzle solved *and* one mistake reviewed), and give a "rest day" freeze so kids don't play tired at midnight to protect a number.
- **Aina, the "Great move!" pop during a game:** no. Praise during play teaches the student to rely on outside approval. Give it in Review.
- **Mascot:** only if it delivers the coach line. A bird that dances on wins is decoration.
- **Farid, "numbers before words":** right for you, wrong as the default for a 600-rated player. Make it a setting.
- **Brilliant/Great labels:** I agree with Farid that they're noise. Keep them, but make them a quiet badge.

## (c) Compromises I'd accept

- A **Review profile** setting (*Learner / Club / Analyst*) that decides labels vs numbers, whether captions show, and whether the engine stays hidden until you self-review. The default comes from your rating, and you can override it.
- In **Practice mode** (unrated), the student can switch on takebacks, the hanging-piece glow and hints (which piece to move, not where). Rated mode is clean.
- **Engine-first review:** Club and Analyst profiles can skip self-review. Learner gets a light version: tap 1–3 moments you weren't sure about before the engine appears.

## (d) My final rankings

**Top 5 features**
1. Mistake-to-puzzle loop with spaced repetition
2. Analysis board + variation tree + position setup (FEN) + lossless PGN
3. Practice vs Rated mode (takebacks, hanging-piece glow, hint)
4. Self-review step + plain-words caption + Try again in Review
5. Bulk import (multi-game PGN, Lichess username)

**Top 3 cosmetics**
1. Threat arrow (red) vs better-move arrow (green), each switchable on its own
2. Badge on the destination square, plus a one-line plain caption under it
3. Plain high-contrast default board, coordinates on, and a "hide rating" toggle

## (e) Proposed build order (serves all four of us)

1. **Analysis board with variation tree + FEN setup + "Continue from here" in Review.** Every later item depends on it: Farid's work, Daniel's what-ifs, my lesson prep, Aina's Try again.
2. **Practice/Rated split on New game,** with takebacks + hanging-piece glow in Practice. This keeps Aina playing and keeps ratings honest.
3. **Review upgrade:** plain caption + Try again + Review profile (Learner / Club / Analyst with numbers and multi-PV) + "Analyse deeper here".
4. **Bulk import + lossless PGN.** This gives the system real games to work on.
5. **"My mistakes" puzzle set with spaced repetition and a trained-days streak.** It brings together everything above, and it's the daily habit for all four of us.
